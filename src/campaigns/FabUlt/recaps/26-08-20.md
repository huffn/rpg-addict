## After the fight
The aftermath of the battle in the Chamber of Hardened Light was quiet, save for the hum of the hard light platform beneath the party's feet. Colton and the Aetherborn were gone without a trace, leaving only the spear wedged deep into the glowing floor as evidence of the confrontation. Krish approached the weapon and, with considerable effort, wrenched the wooden shaft free from the platform, though the spearhead remained stubbornly embedded. He turned the shaft over in his hands, studying the intricate carvings of the Shattered Will motif worked into the grain of the wood, and decided it was worth keeping.
## Return to Dextrin
With the Wind Harmonic claimed and their enemies fled, the party made their way back across the hard light bridge and up the stairs, the River of Shadows churning silently far below them. Chris Anthemum was waiting at the edge of the island, diligently wiping down his sky ferry as though nothing remarkable had happened. He ferried them back toward Dextrin, spiraling lazily through the aetheric currents, and as they traveled, the party watched the world begin to correct itself in dramatic and unsettling fashion. The wind currents that had orbited the harmonic's island slowed and faded entirely, and in the distance, floating islands began to lose their unnatural buoyancy, some settling gently into valleys below, others crashing violently into cliff faces and splitting apart. Dextrin itself held firm, its massive anchoring chains doing exactly what they were built to do.
## Meeting with the Professor
Back in the floating city, the party made their way to the central tower and rode the pneumatic tube up to Room 19, where Professor Alera Vorun sat buried in an impressive tome. She looked up with genuine curiosity when they entered, and her eyes lit up when she saw the Wind Harmonic. She turned it over carefully in her hands, marveling aloud that something so small could power an entire world, before handing it back. Almost as an afterthought, she produced three badly smashed masks — the raccoon-style visors of low-ranking Shattered Will operatives — and set them on the desk, explaining that a group of them had stormed Dextrin shortly after the party had left. She had dealt with them herself, using her books and the room's gravity-shifting architecture to bat them away, and was still picking shards out of her book cover as she spoke.

Alera explained that gathering the harmonics was only the first step. The true goal was breaking into the Savawell Thicket, the source of the red energy pulses that had been troubling researchers for some time. She intended to remain in Dextrin a while longer, as the library there rivaled even the great university back home. She directed the party eastward, into the forested lands of Dhruv, where the next harmonic awaited.
## Parting Gifts
The descent from Dextrin to the ground-level settlement of Dextros was scenic, and Chris Anthemum made the trip with them one final time, spiraling his ferry down along the great anchoring chains. At the edge of the Dhruvian embassy, he stopped them before they could say their goodbyes and reached into a bag. He produced a set of small, carefully chosen stones — a green one for Krish from Dhruv, a sandy one for Sabra from Samitra, a black one for Quinlan, a red one for Vien and the pulses he talked about from the University, and a blue one the color of open sky for Chris himself — explaining that they were shaped like floating islands, for luck, or at least for falling gracefully. He tucked his own matching stone back under his shirt, waved, and sailed away, his voice carrying back to them on the wind as he disappeared into the distance.
## A Message from Dhruv
Inside the embassy, Dexter met them with urgency, pressing a letter into Krish's hands. The wax seal had been applied by trembling fingers, and the contents were no less unsettling. The letter was from Krish's mother, and it described a Dhruv that the party barely recognized. Hunters were remembering songs from childhood. Families were recalling stories they thought lost forever. But the harmless strangeness had curdled into something darker — a woman claimed a tree had spoken to her in her late husband's voice, forest wardens reported laughter echoing through abandoned villages, and entire communities had simply vanished, meals still sitting on tables, tools left in the fields. The most troubling incidents, the letter noted, had all occurred near places touched by Seradin's work. It closed with a line that settled over the group like a cold fog: the forest was remembering out loud.
## Whispers from the Acorn
As Krish processed the letter, Quinlan noticed something strange about the acorn pendant he had reclaimed from Colton. It had grown warm in his hand, and a whisper threaded itself through his mind — a voice that sounded disturbingly like Colton's, saying only that he had carried it north. The party filed the unsettling detail away and turned their attention to the road ahead.
## Preparing to Leave
Getting to Dhruv meant navigating terrain that was no longer entirely trustworthy. Dexter warned them that the roads were shifting, the forest rearranging itself in ways that defied cartography. The party debated their options and settled on following the river tributaries down from the mountains, reasoning that water, at least, knew where it was going. They gathered supplies, traded for foodstuffs, and Quinlan located a hidden stash left by his mentor Fernath in a pre-arranged corner of Dextros. The journey began on foot and by gamel along a steep mountain path that hugged the river's edge, the party carefully watching their footing as the animals navigated the treacherous descent with far more confidence than their riders.
## Voyage of the Sea Dog
When the terrain finally leveled out enough, the party gathered logs and fashioned a raft they christened the Sea Dog. It floated admirably. It did not, however, steer. The attempt to build a functional rudder had failed entirely, and the Sea Dog immediately began corkscrewing down the river at the mercy of the current, spinning lazily as the party clung to their makeshift shelter of coats propped up on Colton's spear shaft. Quinlan abandoned ship in spirit if not in body, leaping between the trees along the bank to scout ahead, while the others rode the spinning raft into increasingly warm and humid air. The rock and snow of the mountains gave way to vegetation, then to smaller forests, and finally to the towering, familiar trees of Dhruv.
## The Silent Village
The river eventually spread into an estuary near the Volund-Dhruv border, grounding the Sea Dog in the shallows. The party made their way toward a nearby village, and from a distance it looked ordinary enough. Up close, it was anything but. Doors hung open. Cooking fires had gone cold. Children's toys lay scattered in the road, and a cart sat half-loaded in the village square as though its owner had simply walked away mid-task. Not a soul was to be found. The fruit trees within the village's spiritual boundary had shriveled into black husks, and the crops in the surrounding fields looked as though they had been dead for a decade. At the center of it all, the Heart Tree stood as a tortured, shriveled corpse, its roots clawing upward from the earth. Etched into those roots were two marks: the symbol of the Shattered Will, and the unmistakable signature of Seradin.

In the lore keeper's hut, the party found a journal chronicling the village's slow decline. Early entries spoke of the land being blessed, of the forest recovering. Later ones noted weakening roots and families choosing to leave. The final entry, written roughly two months prior, read simply: the trees no longer sing. Huddled in the corner of the hut was a drained old elder, the last person left. He explained that the forest had taken the people as a sacrifice — a balancing of accounts for the life force Seradin had siphoned from the Heart Tree. He believed the missing villagers had been drawn toward the capital, Llerrum. The party left him there and pressed on, passing through the similarly hollowed village of Rootmir before trading their ruined raft for canoes and continuing downriver.
## The Whispering Bark
As the river spread into marshland outside the capital, the party abandoned the canoes and continued on foot. It was here that the memory trees made themselves known. When Quinlan placed his hand against the bark of one of the rainbow-hued trees, the wood rippled and the entire party was pulled into a shared vision — a younger Quinlan, alone, practicing blade tricks he had seen the senior assassins perform, nicking himself badly when the knife slipped. The scar he had always claimed was from shaving had a rather less dignified origin. The bark shifted to form the words: We remember. Vien's encounter with a nearby tree was no less revealing, projecting a scene of a young student caught by Professor Vorun smuggling food from the cafeteria, a ruler snapping against the back of his hand. The tree's message read: We always remember.

Krish attempted to use the trees more deliberately, reaching out through the bark to contact his mother. The connection opened like a warm current, and her voice came through clearly, full of love and anticipation, feeling unmistakably like the capital. When he tried to reach his old tutor Han, however, the connection returned nothing but a hollow, barren silence that felt exactly like Rootmir. Sabra's turn came last. The tree showed her younger self in Samitra, performing in a circus alongside her friend Dahlia, reaching out to catch her from a camel and missing entirely, tumbling off her own mount as her mother rushed forward in alarm. The bark formed its final message above her hand: We're always watching.
## The Muted Herald
Far away, in a dark chamber lined with flickering magical panels, Colton knelt before the Muted Herald. Three of the panels now burned steady and still — Galatea, Samitra, and Volund — while the rest strobed with shifting images. Colton reported the loss of the Wind Harmonic. The Herald received the news with cold composure, but when Colton added that the Aetherborn had never returned — no body, no trace, no signal — the Herald paused. He called it unexpected, and for a moment something genuine flickered behind his authority. A lesser operative attempted to interrupt and was silenced with a single raised hand. The Herald turned back to Colton and told him he continued to test his patience. Colton replied that this was nothing new. The scene faded to black. The walls of Llerrum rose before the party as the session drew to a close, the capital of Dhruv waiting just beyond the gate.
