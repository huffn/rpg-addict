## A Companion of Questionable Trust
The party stood on the side street outside the Shattered Will's southern cell, Jasmin at their side and Tessel Rootbinder a careful step behind. Before deciding anything, they pressed Tessel on what she knew. Father Solivar, she told them, was the leader of the faith inside the Radiant Seat, and he was Shattered Will. Not everyone in the Seat was, but the order had worked its way inside. Whatever they were looking for, she said, would be in the beacon: the great tower rising above the cathedral, where Solivar kept the things that mattered to him. The party argued briefly over whether to bring her and settled it with grim practicality: if she came along, at least they could watch her stab them in the back. Tessel answered by slipping her Voice mask into the lining of Quinlan's cloak. She didn't need it anymore. They left Jasmin safely at the inn and set out across the city.
## The First Bell
In the Radiant Seat it is always noon, and the bells still mark the hours. As the bell rang for the first noon of the day, every bag holding a Harmonic jerked toward the cathedral like a hand tugging at a sleeve, and a faint answering ring sounded from inside them. The night-shift crowd in the plaza saw that something strange had happened. Any hope of sneaking in vanished when a choir boy ran up, breathless, to announce that Father Solivar wished to speak with them. His satchel was stuffed with their wanted posters.

The cathedral doors stood open this time. Solivar was in the middle of a sermon about rooting out corruption, and his congregation hardly glanced at the newcomers before answering him in their flat, unfeeling voice: "By order of the Light-Bearer Council, the Light holds." "And the Light is still." Then the crowd filed out, and Solivar beckoned the party forward. His offer was smooth and generous. The Harmonics would be far safer in the Seat's keeping than dragged across the world, and if the party handed them over, he would speak to the rest of the Council and make the matter of the posters disappear. The party wasn't buying. If the Seat cared so much about protecting the Harmonics, they asked, why had it never gone to get them? Vien watched him closely and saw that he did not have the Light Harmonic. He knew where it was, but he couldn't reach it. When talk turned to the Harmonics, his eyes flicked toward a heavy oak door at the back of the cathedral, the way into the beacon, and it plainly galled him. "You don't have it, do you?" came the quiet jab. "And you can't get it." Solivar's composure cracked. He threw up his hands and swept out of the cathedral with a parting warning: "The beacon is not a door. Some light is meant to stay sealed."
## The Door That Was a Door
The door behind the altar was a different matter. It was massive oak bound in metal, with a hint of some technology none of them understood, and it was locked, with no keyhole anywhere on their side. Vien dredged up enough lore about doors like it to work out that it opened inward: a push door, not a pull. Krish turned his entropic magic on the wood and aged it years in a few seconds until the timbers began to give. Quinlan went for his lock picks and found nothing to pick. In the end Sabra simply put her shoulder into it and rammed the door open with a crash. The noise was hardly subtle, but she propped it back on its hinges so carefully that only a close inspection would show anything had happened.

Beyond lay a spiral staircase winding up a very tall tower. As they climbed, every brazier along the wall flared as the Harmonics passed and dimmed again behind them, marking their progress in fire. After what felt like an endless climb, the stairs opened into a chamber beneath the great bell. A table stood in the middle of the room, and beside it stood Father Solivar, who had somehow arrived first. "I did tell you," he said. "The beacon is not a door."
## The Voice at the Table
As Solivar raised his hands, two towering mirrors of hard light lifted off the table and took their places at his flanks. Krish opened with Rootbreaker, cutting at all three at once, but one mirror slid in front of its master and took the blow meant for him. Solivar began gathering the beacon's light into the mirrors, and the party soon learned what that meant: at the end of each exchange the stored light burst across the whole room. Vien wrapped his companions in Soul Shroud, and between that, Krish's Wyrmglass Scale and Quinlan's black cloak, all four of them could shrug off half the glare. Solivar singled out Sabra instead, and though she halved the light like the rest, his searing rebukes and the mirror's dazzling glare kept landing, leaving her dazed and wearing her down. Quinlan landed a pair of perfect shots, and the mirrors' formation began to waver.

The answer was the mirrors themselves. Sabra spent her turn wrenching them out of alignment, Quinlan rained potions to restore his allies' magic, and Vien poured out healing. Then Krish trusted his luck, called on Lucky Seven, and turned the last mirror away from Solivar. The light building for the next blast guttered out. Solivar cast Stillness ("Stillness is mercy"), and Sabra threw herself into its path. Then, tired of the mirrors, she smashed the first one to pieces, earning herself seven years' bad luck, and soon afterward the second, doubling it to fourteen. Vien's magic left Solivar shaken, and Quinlan's dark-edged shots tore into a man who turned out to be badly vulnerable to darkness. Quinlan tore a rosary from Solivar with a soul-stealing strike, an odd ornament that looked almost like a turtle. Krish and Sabra pressed in, and Sabra landed the final blow. As it struck home, Solivar caught hold of the hard light spilling from the shattered mirrors and was gone, streaking out of the tower along it like a bolt.
## A Map of a Lost World
Broken glass littered the floor, and with Solivar gone the party finally had a good look at the table. A map of the world was carved into it, laid out with fixed blocks, light-catching triangles and a prism to one side, and mirrors could be set and turned across its surface to bend the beacon's light. Their Harmonics resonated as they leaned over it. They placed the Harmonics they carried on their places on the map, worked out by color and region, then angled mirror after mirror until the light reached every one. The table shook, and the fifth Harmonic, the Light, rose from it. When Sabra took it, every light on the table drew together onto a single point.

Quinlan was the first to notice that the map was wrong, or rather old. A symbol had appeared and was pulsing where none had been carved before, and the coastline around it matched no map any of them knew. Looking at it, Vien saw again in his mind's eye the vision he had caught Seradin stealing in Dhruv: an enormous city at the heart of a forest larger than Dhruv, then something opening and white light swallowing everything. He felt sure the two were connected. Thinking back, he remembered the history-of-the-world course every first-year at Lux Arcanum had to survive. Hundreds of years ago something terrible had happened, and part of the Broken had been wiped off the face of the earth. Half of it sank, and no one had ever had many answers. On any modern map, that spot was open water. The party agreed that the missing place was where they had to go, by ship or by something stranger.

Searching the room, they turned up only mirror glass from Solivar's side of the fight, but a drawer in the table held a surprise: a survey lens, part of a survey lantern, stamped as the property of Lux Arcanum. Vien knew the type from his classes. Such lenses measure the strength of ley lines in the soulstream, marking where magic runs strongest, and work far better than the dowsing rods most people use. Vien kept it. What one was doing locked in a drawer at the top of the Radiant Seat's beacon, nobody could say.
## The Shadow Moves
Tessel had spent the fight hidden in the stairwell, unseen by Solivar. The party made their way back down and out through the cathedral, only to find the city in uproar. A strange darkness had fallen while they were inside, and though it had passed, something had changed. For twenty years the sun had hung in one place, and the shadows had burned their outlines into the pale granite pavements. Now the shadow on the ground no longer matched its burned outline. The sun had moved. At the inn, Jasmin had news of her own: she had gone back to the ruins of the embassy and recovered a ledger naming several of the Council's clerks.

Far away, in a dark chamber of arched windows, the panes showing Samitra, Dhruv, Galatea and the Volund Etherium had stopped their flickering, and a fifth now held steady on the Radiant Seat. Seradin stepped into the room, alive, cradling Mr. Bigglesworth, and found the Muted Herald's throne empty. Father Solivar emerged from the shadows, his robes scorched and torn and a shield-shaped dent in his face. "He has gone ahead," Solivar said. "You may give it to me." Seradin hesitated, then handed over the memory he had stolen from Dhruv. "Then they will go to him," said Solivar. "Let them."
## Your Own Eyes
Back at the inn, Tessel made the party an offer. "Everyone in this city sees what the Light wants them to see," she said. "Let me give you something no one here has: your own eyes." One by one she asked them to look at her and touched their foreheads, and a memory each of them had carried since Llerrum cracked like glass. Krish's final blow, Seradin bound and dragged off to the dungeon: none of it had happened. In its place came the truth. Seradin had stood up with a hand at his temple and walked out of the Central Grove right between the four of them, smiling, while they stared at nothing. "He was never in that cell," Tessel said. "But now he can't make you believe that he was." Whatever Seradin did next, he would no longer be rewriting their memories to do it.
