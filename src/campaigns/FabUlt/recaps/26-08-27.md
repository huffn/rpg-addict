## Arrival at Llerrum's Gates
The party arrived at the outskirts of Llerrum, the capital city of Dhruv, with its great walls finally visible on the horizon. Before they could pass through the gates, however, they spotted something deeply unsettling — three figures in white robes and gold-marked half-face masks wandering aimlessly just outside the city. One walked in slow, confused circles, another stared blankly at the grass and then up at the treeline as if unable to make sense of the world around him, and the third sat cross-legged on the ground, hollow-eyed and vacant. As Krish strode toward them with barely contained fury, one of the operatives began to mutter fragments of thought aloud, and in an instant, lucidity snapped back into all three of their faces. They recognized the party from wanted posters, and the skirmish began.
## Skirmish with the Brute Squad
The fight was fierce and unrelenting. The largest of the three — a hulking brute the party came to think of as Fezzik — wielded a lightning-charged baton that crackled with bolt energy, striking multiple members of the party in wide, punishing arcs. Krish opened the assault with a powerful slash, while Quinlan unleashed a rapid barrage of light energy that struck two of the operatives at once. Sabra moved like a shield made flesh, throwing herself in front of blows meant for her allies and answering each strike with one of her own. Vien worked tirelessly from the back, mending wounds and restoring the party's strength as the operatives hammered them with relentless magical bolt attacks.

The tide nearly turned against the party when a devastating coordinated assault from the operatives overwhelmed Krish, dropping him unconscious to the ground. The situation looked grim, but Vien refused to let his companion fall. Drawing deep on his reserves of magical energy — nearly draining himself completely — he channeled a powerful surge of restorative spirit magic into Krish, rekindling the flame of courage within him and pulling him back from the edge. Krish rose, shaken but furious, and immediately rejoined the fight.

From there, the party surged back with renewed ferocity. Quinlan lined up a precise, lethal shot with Telestria's Demise and brought Fezzik crashing down. Vien unleashed a torrent of fire that engulfed the remaining two operatives, dropping one of them where he stood. The last operative, desperate and spent, poured every last drop of his magical energy into one final devastating blast before Sabra stepped forward and ended the fight with a decisive blow. The party collected the fallen operatives' gold-marked masks, summoned the city guards, and had the captives hauled off to the dungeon beneath the palace.
## The Quiet Capital
Walking through the streets of Llerrum, the party noticed immediately that something was wrong. The city was open for business — shops were lit, guards were at their posts — but the streets were eerily quiet, the foot traffic barely half of what Krish remembered from his time growing up there. A heavy, unnatural stillness had settled over the capital, and no one seemed to be able to explain it. Krish led the group briskly through the palace doors and straight to the throne room, where his mother, the Queen of Dhruv, rose to greet him with warmth and relief.
## Audience with the Queen
The reunion, however, quickly revealed the depth of the crisis. The Queen believed Krish had only been gone two months, her sense of time quietly distorted. When Krish pressed her about the state of the kingdom and the harmonics, she reached instinctively for a locket at her throat — a memento of the Dhruvian harmonic, kept close — but when he produced the urgent letter she had sent him, she stared at her own handwriting in silence and admitted she had no memory of writing it. Her memory had not been erased entirely, but carved into selectively, specific moments and truths quietly hollowed out. Krish embraced her, ensured she was safe, and then led the party toward the Central Grove.
## The Sigils of the Central Grove
The Central Grove was a vast forested park at the heart of Llerrum, a place where the city had grown up around ancient trees rather than cutting them down. Its outer edges were carefully tended, but as the party moved deeper, the manicured gardens gave way to wilder, older growth. Krish noticed that while many of the trees looked strangely, almost unnaturally healthy, others bore the unmistakable signs of magical malnourishment — and etched into the roots of those afflicted trees, repeating every three feet in a perfect ring around the grove, were arcane sigils. Vien recognized them immediately as containment runes, the kind studied in advanced magical theory, and the party began to piece together a grim picture: the ritual was not keeping something out. It was keeping something in — or more precisely, draining something from within.
## The Withered Mentor
Before they could reach the grove's inner sanctum, they came across a figure crawling slowly across the ground, pulling himself toward the threshold with trembling arms. Krish ran to him and, with a sinking heart, recognized the eyes of Han, his beloved mentor — though Han now looked thirty years older than when Krish had last seen him, his body withered and his strength nearly gone. Han warned them in a ragged voice that Seradin was inside the grove, harvesting memories, experience, and identity from the ancient roots. Krish gave him water from his skin, helped him to rest against a tree, promised to return, and then turned back toward the grove with a look that left no room for hesitation.
## Seradin's Data Center
Descending a short flight of stairs into the subterranean chamber beneath the grove, the party found a room that had been transformed beyond recognition. Where once there had been only the natural beauty of ancient roots forming a vaulted ceiling, there now stood a massive machine, its mechanisms driven by tree roots that had been forcefully shoved into its workings. The grove had been turned into something monstrous — a device for extracting and storing the memories and wisdom of an entire people. And there, seated in a swivel chair before the machine, stroking a cat with practiced calm, was Seradin. He turned slowly to face them and asked, almost philosophically, how much wisdom they thought sat unused in the roots of the forest. Krish met his gaze without flinching and told him plainly that nothing here belonged to him. Seradin smiled, and the air between them crackled with the promise of violence.
